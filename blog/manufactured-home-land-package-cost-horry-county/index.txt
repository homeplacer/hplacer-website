1f:T1927,<p>If you&#39;ve started pricing out a new home around the Grand Strand, you&#39;ve probably run into the same confusing wall everyone does. One website quotes you a home price. Another quotes you land. Somebody else mentions delivery, and a fourth person starts talking about septic and power hookups. By the time you add it all up in your head, you have no idea what the real number is — or whether you can afford it.</p>
<p>Here&#39;s the honest version of how it works, and why buying a new home and the land it sits on as one package is usually the cleaner, cheaper way to do it here in Horry County.</p>
<h2>The &quot;package&quot; model, in plain English</h2>
<p>When we talk about a land-home package, we mean exactly that: a brand-new manufactured home from a builder like Clayton, Cavco, or Champion, set on a piece of land you own, ready to live in. One deal. One closing. One number.</p>
<p>A typical package around Conway, Loris, Longs, Aynor, or the outskirts of Myrtle Beach includes:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The home itself</strong> — new construction, your floor plan, your finishes.</li>
<li><strong>A lot</strong> — often around a quarter-acre, give or take, with no HOA telling you what color to paint the shutters.</li>
<li><strong>Delivery and set</strong> — getting the home to the site and properly installed and anchored.</li>
<li><strong>Utilities and site work</strong> — the well or water tap, septic or sewer connection, power, and the pad the home sits on.</li>
</ul>
<p>That last bullet is the one people forget, and it&#39;s the one that wrecks budgets when folks try to buy the pieces separately. A home price by itself is not a move-in price. The package price gets you to move-in.</p>
<h2>So what does it actually cost?</h2>
<p>Most new land-home packages in our area start in the <strong>low $200s</strong> and move up from there depending on the home&#39;s size, the floor plan, and the specific lot. A bigger home, a nicer kitchen, or a more desirable piece of land moves the number up. A smaller, efficient plan on a standard lot keeps it down.</p>
<p>We&#39;re not going to throw a fake &quot;starting at&quot; price at you to get you in the door and then surprise you later. Land cost moves with location and lot size. Site work moves with what the ground needs — some lots are ready to go, others need more grading or a longer utility run. The fastest way to get a real number for a real home on a real lot is to call us at <strong>(843) 849-HOME</strong> and we&#39;ll walk it with you.</p>
<p>What we <em>can</em> tell you plainly: the price you should care about is the all-in, on-the-land, ready-to-live-in number — not the sticker on the home alone. Always ask anyone you talk to whether their quote includes the land and the site work, because a lot of quotes out there don&#39;t.</p>
<h2>Why bundling beats buying the pieces separately</h2>
<p>You <em>can</em> buy a home from one place, find land on your own, and hire out the delivery, the well, the septic, and the electrical separately. People do it. But it&#39;s usually slower, more stressful, and more expensive. Here&#39;s why the package wins:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>No coordination headaches.</strong> Buying separately means you&#39;re the general contractor whether you want to be or not. You&#39;re chasing the land seller, the home dealer, the well company, the septic installer, and the power company — and if one of them is late, everybody waits. In a package, that coordination is our job, not yours.</li>
<li><strong>Fewer surprise costs.</strong> When the pieces are quoted separately, the gaps between them are where budgets blow up. Who&#39;s paying for the culvert? The permit? The final grading? In a package, those line items live in one quote, so there&#39;s nothing hiding in the cracks.</li>
<li><strong>One financing path.</strong> This is the big one. Trying to finance raw land, a home, and improvements as three separate transactions is a paperwork nightmare. As a package, a new home on land can often be financed as a single home loan — and these typically qualify for <strong>FHA, VA, or conventional financing</strong> rather than the higher-rate, shorter-term loans people associate with older mobile homes.</li>
<li><strong>It appraises and holds value like a home.</strong> A new, permanently-set home on land you own is real property — not a vehicle title in a rented park. That matters for your loan, your taxes, and your resale down the road.</li>
</ul>
<h2>What you own at the end — and the warranty</h2>
<p>When the dust settles, you own the home <em>and</em> the land underneath it. No lot rent. No HOA. No landlord deciding to sell the park out from under you. For a lot of first-time buyers around here, that&#39;s the whole point — a real foundation, on real ground, with payments that often land near or below what they were paying to rent.</p>
<p>And because these are brand-new homes, they come with a <strong>one-year warranty</strong>. If something&#39;s off in that first year, it gets handled. That&#39;s a very different experience from buying a used home as-is and hoping for the best.</p>
<h2>A realistic way to think about your budget</h2>
<p>If you&#39;re trying to ballpark this before you call, a sensible approach is:</p>
<ol>
<li>Start with the low $200s as a floor for a new package in our area.</li>
<li>Add for size and floor plan — more square footage and more bedrooms cost more.</li>
<li>Add for the land — a larger or more in-demand lot costs more than a standard quarter-acre out toward Loris or Aynor.</li>
<li>Factor your financing — your rate, your down payment, and your loan type (FHA, VA, conventional) shape the monthly payment as much as the price does.</li>
</ol>
<p>Then, instead of guessing, get a real quote on a real home and lot. That&#39;s the only number that actually matters.</p>
