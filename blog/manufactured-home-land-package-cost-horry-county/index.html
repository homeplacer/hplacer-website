What a New Home on Land Really Costs in Horry County

A plain-spoken breakdown of what a new manufactured home on land costs in Horry County, SC — the package model, what's bundled in, and why it beats buying the pieces separately.

If you've started pricing out a new home around the Grand Strand, you've probably run into the same confusing wall everyone does. One website quotes you a home price. Another quotes you land. Somebody else mentions delivery, and a fourth person starts talking about septic and power hookups. By the time you add it all up in your head, you have no idea what the real number is — or whether you can afford it.

Here's the honest version of how it works, and why buying a new home and the land it sits on as one package is usually the cleaner, cheaper way to do it here in Horry County.

The "package" model, in plain English

When we talk about a land-home package, we mean exactly that: a brand-new manufactured home from a builder like Clayton, Cavco, or Champion, set on a piece of land you own, ready to live in. One deal. One closing. One number.

A typical package around Conway, Loris, Longs, Aynor, or the outskirts of Myrtle Beach includes:

  • The home itself — new construction, your floor plan, your finishes.
  • A lot — often around a quarter-acre, give or take, with no HOA telling you what color to paint the shutters.
  • Delivery and set — getting the home to the site and properly installed and anchored.
  • Utilities and site work — the well or water tap, septic or sewer connection, power, and the pad the home sits on.

That last bullet is the one people forget, and it's the one that wrecks budgets when folks try to buy the pieces separately. A home price by itself is not a move-in price. The package price gets you to move-in.

So what does it actually cost?

Most new land-home packages in our area start in the low $200s and move up from there depending on the home's size, the floor plan, and the specific lot. A bigger home, a nicer kitchen, or a more desirable piece of land moves the number up. A smaller, efficient plan on a standard lot keeps it down.

We're not going to throw a fake "starting at" price at you to get you in the door and then surprise you later. Land cost moves with location and lot size. Site work moves with what the ground needs — some lots are ready to go, others need more grading or a longer utility run. The fastest way to get a real number for a real home on a real lot is to call us at (843) 849-HOME and we'll walk it with you.

What we can tell you plainly: the price you should care about is the all-in, on-the-land, ready-to-live-in number — not the sticker on the home alone. Always ask anyone you talk to whether their quote includes the land and the site work, because a lot of quotes out there don't.

Why bundling beats buying the pieces separately

You can buy a home from one place, find land on your own, and hire out the delivery, the well, the septic, and the electrical separately. People do it. But it's usually slower, more stressful, and more expensive. Here's why the package wins:

  • No coordination headaches. Buying separately means you're the general contractor whether you want to be or not. You're chasing the land seller, the home dealer, the well company, the septic installer, and the power company — and if one of them is late, everybody waits. In a package, that coordination is our job, not yours.
  • Fewer surprise costs. When the pieces are quoted separately, the gaps between them are where budgets blow up. Who's paying for the culvert? The permit? The final grading? In a package, those line items live in one quote, so there's nothing hiding in the cracks.
  • One financing path. This is the big one. Trying to finance raw land, a home, and improvements as three separate transactions is a paperwork nightmare. As a package, a new home on land can often be financed as a single home loan — and these typically qualify for FHA, VA, or conventional financing rather than the higher-rate, shorter-term loans people associate with older mobile homes.
  • It appraises and holds value like a home. A new, permanently-set home on land you own is real property — not a vehicle title in a rented park. That matters for your loan, your taxes, and your resale down the road.

What you own at the end — and the warranty

When the dust settles, you own the home and the land underneath it. No lot rent. No HOA. No landlord deciding to sell the park out from under you. For a lot of first-time buyers around here, that's the whole point — a real foundation, on real ground, with payments that often land near or below what they were paying to rent.

And because these are brand-new homes, they come with a one-year warranty. If something's off in that first year, it gets handled. That's a very different experience from buying a used home as-is and hoping for the best.

A realistic way to think about your budget

If you're trying to ballpark this before you call, a sensible approach is:

  1. Start with the low $200s as a floor for a new package in our area.
  2. Add for size and floor plan — more square footage and more bedrooms cost more.
  3. Add for the land — a larger or more in-demand lot costs more than a standard quarter-acre out toward Loris or Aynor.
  4. Factor your financing — your rate, your down payment, and your loan type (FHA, VA, conventional) shape the monthly payment as much as the price does.

Then, instead of guessing, get a real quote on a real home and lot. That's the only number that actually matters.
