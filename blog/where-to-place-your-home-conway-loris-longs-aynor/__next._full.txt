1f:T1ade,<p>Picking the house is the fun part. But anybody who&#39;s shopped for a place to live around here knows the real question that keeps you up at night is <em>where</em>. The same floor plan can feel completely different sitting on a quiet country lot outside Aynor versus a few minutes from the Conway riverfront. And since we sell the home and the land it sits on as one package, where you land is a decision worth slowing down for.</p>
<p>The good news is that Horry County gives you room to choose. You can be a short drive from the beach or settled into pine-and-pasture country, and a lot of the land in between still has space to spread out. Here&#39;s an honest tour of the towns we serve, so you can get a feel for each before you ever step onto a lot.</p>
<h2>First, How the Land Part Works</h2>
<p>Before we drive around, it helps to know what you&#39;re actually buying. When you go through Home Placer, you get a brand-new home from Clayton, Cavco, or Champion <strong>plus the land underneath it</strong>, in one closing. You own the dirt.</p>
<p>We also handle the parts most people don&#39;t want to think about:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Permits</strong> with the county</li>
<li><strong>Delivery and setup</strong> of the home</li>
<li><strong>The foundation</strong> it sits on</li>
<li><strong>Utilities</strong> — well or water, septic or sewer, and power</li>
</ul>
<p>Lots around here are often somewhere in the neighborhood of a quarter-acre, give or take, though land comes in all shapes and sizes. The point is, you&#39;re not just buying a house. You&#39;re choosing a piece of Horry County to call your own.</p>
<h2>Conway: Close to Everything, Still Its Own Place</h2>
<p>Conway sits inland along the Waccamaw River, and it&#39;s the seat of the county, so it tends to feel like a real town rather than a turnoff. You&#39;ve got the historic downtown, the river walk, shopping, and easy connections out to the rest of the area.</p>
<p>For a homebuyer, Conway&#39;s appeal is <strong>balance</strong>. You can find land that&#39;s tucked back and quiet but still be a reasonable drive from groceries, work, and the medical and shopping corridors. And when you do want sand between your toes, Myrtle Beach is a manageable trip down the highway — close enough for a weekend habit, far enough that you&#39;re not living in the tourist crush.</p>
<p>If you want some breathing room without feeling cut off from the conveniences, Conway is usually the first place we&#39;d point you to look.</p>
<h2>Loris: Quiet, Open, and Unhurried</h2>
<p>Head up toward the northern part of the county and you reach Loris, where the pace slows down and the land opens up. This is farm-and-forest country — the kind of place where lots tend to come with more elbow room and the night sky actually gets dark.</p>
<p>Loris is a good fit if what you&#39;re after is <strong>space and quiet</strong>. You&#39;re trading a longer drive to the oceanfront for a more rural setting, bigger skies, and generally more land to work with. If you like the idea of a garden, a workshop, or just not hearing the neighbors, the area around Loris has a lot to offer.</p>
<p>It&#39;s farther from the beach than Conway, no question. But &quot;farther&quot; out here is still a drive, not an expedition, and plenty of people decide the trade is well worth it.</p>
<h2>Longs: Country Feel, Beach Within Reach</h2>
<p>Longs sits in the northeastern corner of the county, and it threads a nice needle. You get a genuinely rural, low-key feel, but you&#39;re positioned toward the North Myrtle Beach side of the coast — so the ocean is closer than the country setting might lead you to expect.</p>
<p>That combination is the whole pitch for Longs:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A relaxed, spread-out setting</strong> with land that still has some room</li>
<li><strong>Shorter beach runs</strong> than you&#39;d get from the deeper-inland towns</li>
<li>A location that&#39;s growing but hasn&#39;t lost its country bones</li>
</ul>
<p>If you&#39;ve been daydreaming about a quiet lot where you can still get to the coast without making a day of it, Longs deserves a spot on your list.</p>
<h2>Aynor: The Farthest Out, the Most Wide-Open</h2>
<p>West of Conway, Aynor is the most rural of the towns we serve. This is honest-to-goodness countryside — fields, farmland, two-lane roads, and lots that tend to be generous with space.</p>
<p>Aynor is for the buyer who genuinely wants to <strong>get away from the noise</strong>. You&#39;ll have the longest drive to the beach of any town on this tour, but in exchange you get the most open, unhurried setting and, often, the most land for the money. If your idea of home includes a porch, a long driveway, and not much traffic going by, Aynor is worth the trip out to see.</p>
<h2>A Quick Word on Today&#39;s Homes</h2>
<p>Wherever you land, it&#39;s worth saying plainly: the homes we place out here are not the &quot;trailers&quot; of decades past. Modern HUD-code manufactured homes from builders like Clayton, Cavco, and Champion come with <strong>real drywall, energy-efficient construction, and genuine floor plans</strong> that live like any other new house.</p>
<p>You&#39;re also covered. Every home comes with a full 1-year home warranty, <em>plus</em> a 2-10 warranty — coverage on systems like plumbing, electrical, and HVAC for the first two years and on major structural items for ten. There&#39;s a 30-day walk-through after you move in, too, so anything that needs attention gets caught early.</p>
<h2>Figuring Out What You Can Do</h2>
<p>Location and budget go hand in hand, and that&#39;s where it pays to talk to a real lender. We&#39;re not a lender ourselves, but we connect buyers to ones who handle this every day. A few things worth knowing as you think about the map:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>USDA Rural Development</strong> loans offer $0 down in eligible rural areas — and a lot of the land on the outskirts of Conway, Loris, Longs, and Aynor often qualifies (income limits apply; it has to be your primary residence).</li>
<li><strong>VA loans</strong> offer $0 down for eligible veterans.</li>
<li><strong>FHA</strong> can mean as little as about 3.5% down.</li>
</ul>
<p>Where you choose to put down roots can actually change which financing doors open, which is one more reason to start the conversation early. Homes around here start from the low $200s, but the right number for you depends on the land, the home, and the loan — so the honest answer is that it varies, and a real quote beats a guess every time.</p>
