Buying Land for Your Manufactured Home on the Grand Strand

A plain-spoken guide to buying land for a manufactured home in Horry County, SC — zoning, utilities, perc tests, access, and how Home Placer handles permits and setup.

A brand-new manufactured home is only half the story. The other half is the ground it sits on. Around the Grand Strand — Myrtle Beach, Conway, Loris, Longs, Aynor — the right lot can make your whole project smoother and cheaper, and the wrong lot can turn into a string of surprises. If you're thinking about putting a Clayton, Cavco, or Champion home on a piece of Carolina land, here's an honest look at what actually matters, and how we handle the parts most folks dread.

What to Look For in a Lot

Not every pretty piece of land is a good piece of land to build on. Before you fall in love with a lot, walk through a short checklist.

Zoning. This is the first thing to confirm, every time. Horry County and the towns inside it each have their own rules about where a manufactured home is allowed and what size or type can go where. Some parcels are zoned for it outright. Others sit in areas with restrictions, or have old deed covenants that quietly limit what you can place. A lot that's perfect on paper does you no good if the county won't permit a home on it. The good news: we check zoning for you before anyone signs anything.

Utilities. Ask how the lot gets water, power, and sewer. Some Grand Strand lots tie into public water and sewer, which is the simplest path. Many rural parcels near Loris, Aynor, and Longs run on a private well and a septic system instead. Neither is wrong — but they change your timeline and your budget, so you want to know up front. Power is usually a matter of how far the nearest line runs and what the utility charges to bring service to your home site.

Perc test and septic. If there's no public sewer, the land needs a septic system, and that means a percolation ("perc") test. A soil evaluator checks how well the ground drains. Sandy, well-draining Carolina soil usually perks fine. Low, wet, or clay-heavy ground may need an engineered system, which costs more. This one matters because you can't skip it — a failed perc test can stop a project cold, so it's worth knowing the soil before you commit.

Access and the home site itself. Can a truck physically get the home onto the lot? A long, narrow, tree-lined drive or a soft, low spot can complicate delivery and setup. You also want enough cleared, level, buildable area for the home, the driveway, and the septic field — all on the same parcel, out of any flood-prone zone. A wooded acre sounds great until you price the clearing and grading.

None of this is meant to scare you off. Most lots around here work just fine. The point is to look before you leap, and to have someone in your corner who knows what they're looking at.

Bring Your Own Land vs. One of Our Lots

There are two clean ways to do this, and we're glad to go either direction.

Bring your own land. Maybe it's been in the family. Maybe you already bought a parcel you love, or you found one for sale near where your people are. If you've got land, we'll put a home on it. We'll vet the lot first — zoning, utilities, perc, access — so we both know it's a buildable site, then handle the home, permits, and setup from there.

Use one of our lots. This is the easiest path for a lot of first-time buyers, and it's really what Home Placer is built around. We pair a brand-new manufactured home with land as one package — one price, one process, one team. The lots we offer have already been checked out, so you're not guessing about whether the ground will perk or whether the county will permit. Our packaged homes start in the low $200s with no HOA, and they qualify for FHA, VA, and conventional financing. Because the home and land come together, your lender sees one clean deal instead of two moving pieces, which usually makes financing simpler too.

There's no wrong choice between the two. If you already have land you're attached to, use it. If you'd rather skip the homework and move into a ready, vetted site, that's exactly what our packages are for. We'll lay out both options honestly and let you pick.

How Home Placer Handles Permits and Setup

This is the part people worry about most, and it's the part we take off your plate.

Once your lot is confirmed and your home is chosen, we manage the permitting through Horry County or the local town — the zoning and building permits, the septic permit and perc coordination if you're on septic, and the utility connections. We schedule delivery and the set: the home is placed on its foundation, leveled, blocked, anchored, and tied down to South Carolina's standards so it stands up to coastal weather. Then comes the button-up work — skirting, steps, utility hookups, and the inspections that sign off the home as ready to live in.

You don't need to become an expert in any of it. That's our job. And because these are brand-new homes, every one comes with a one-year warranty, so if something needs attention after move-in, you've got a number to call and a team that answers.
