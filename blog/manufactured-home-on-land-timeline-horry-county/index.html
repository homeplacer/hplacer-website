From Pick to Keys: Your Home-on-Land Timeline

A realistic, step-by-step look at how long it takes to place a brand-new manufactured home on land in Horry County, SC, and what Home Placer handles for you.

When you decide you want a brand-new home on your own piece of land, the very first question that usually pops up is a simple one: how long is this going to take? You're picturing a moving truck, but you have no idea what stands between today and the day you actually turn the key. That's fair. Putting a new home on land has a few more moving parts than buying a finished house off the market, and nobody walks you through it ahead of time.

So let's walk through it. Here's a realistic look at the whole path, from the home you pick to the keys in your hand, including what tends to take time and what we handle so you don't have to.

Step 1: Choosing your home

This is the fun part, and it can move as fast or as slow as you want. You'll look at floor plans from the builders we carry, Clayton, Cavco, and Champion, and narrow down what fits how you actually live. Number of bedrooms, single-wide or double-wide, kitchen layout, the finishes you want to see every morning.

A few things that shape your timeline here:

  • Decisiveness. Some folks know within a day. Others want to walk a few models and sleep on it. Both are fine.
  • Stock vs. ordered. A home that's ready to go moves faster than one built to your spec from the factory. A factory-ordered home means a build window, which adds time but gets you exactly what you chose.
  • Options and decor. Picking colors, cabinets, and upgrades is quick, but it's worth doing thoughtfully since you'll live with it for years.

Keep in mind these are modern HUD-code homes: drywall, energy-efficient construction, real floor plans. Not the "trailers" people remember from decades ago.

Step 2: Land and financing, working in parallel

Here's where a lot of the real timeline lives, and here's the good news: these two things usually happen at the same time, not one after the other.

On the land side, we sell the home and the land it sits on as one package, with one closing. So you're not out hunting for a lot on your own and then trying to make a separate home deal work. We help match you to land across Horry County, including Myrtle Beach, Conway, Loris, Longs, and Aynor, often around a quarter-acre, give or take. You own the land.

On the financing side, the smart move is to get pre-qualified early so you know your number before you fall in love with anything. We're not a lender, but we connect you to lenders who work with home-and-land packages, and there are a few common roads:

  • USDA Rural Development — $0 down for eligible rural areas, and much of Horry County qualifies (the outskirts of Conway, Loris, Longs, and Aynor often do). Income and area eligibility rules apply, and it has to be your primary residence.
  • VA — $0 down for eligible veterans.
  • FHA — as little as about 3.5% down.

How long this stage takes depends mostly on you and your lender: how quickly you gather pay stubs, tax returns, and the rest of the paperwork. Get your documents together early and this part stops being a bottleneck.

Step 3: Permits and site prep

Once the home is chosen and financing is lining up, the behind-the-scenes work begins, and this is the part most buyers never see, because we handle it.

Placing a home on land in Horry County means county permits, site evaluation, and getting the lot ready. We manage the permitting, schedule the work, and coordinate the trades. This stage involves the county's own timelines, which we don't control, so it's usually a matter of weeks rather than days. The honest truth is permits and inspections move at the pace they move, but you're not the one sitting on hold with the county or chasing down a surveyor. We are.

Step 4: Delivery and set

Now things get exciting. Your home gets delivered to the lot and "set," positioned and secured on its foundation. For a double-wide, that means joining the sections and buttoning up the marriage line so it reads as one seamless home inside.

What's happening during set:

  • The home is placed and leveled on its foundation
  • Sections are joined (for multi-section homes)
  • Exterior is closed up and finished where the pieces meet

The set itself doesn't take long once the crew is on site. The scheduling around it, making sure the lot is ready and the delivery window lines up, is the part we coordinate.

Step 5: Utilities, foundation, and final connections

A home isn't a home until you can flip a switch and run the water. So this stage ties everything together: the foundation work, and the utilities, well or public water, septic or sewer, and power. Depending on the lot, that can mean drilling a well and installing a septic system, or tapping into existing service.

This is another stretch where county and utility-provider timelines come into play, and it's another stretch we manage end to end. You don't need to know the difference between a perc test and a meter set. That's our job. You just need to know it's getting done.

Step 6: Walk-through and keys

Before you move in, we do a final walk-through together. You see everything, top to bottom, and we note anything that needs a touch-up. Then you get your keys.

And you're not on your own after that. Your home comes with:

  • A full 1-year warranty on the home itself
  • A 2-10 Home Buyers Warranty, the national new-home warranty, roughly 2 years on systems like plumbing, electrical, and HVAC, and 10 years on major structural items
  • A 30-day walk-through after you've moved in, so anything that surfaces once you're actually living there gets addressed

So how long, really?

Start to finish, placing a new home on land is usually a matter of a few months, not the weekend a lot of people imagine, but a lot more manageable than building a stick-built house from scratch. The pieces that stretch the timeline are mostly outside anyone's full control: how fast your financing paperwork comes together, the county's permit and inspection schedule, and utility hookups.

The pieces you'd dread, permits, delivery, set, foundation, well and septic, power, inspections, those aren't yours to wrangle. We carry them. Homes start from the low $200s, and the cleanest way to get a real timeline for your situation is to tell us what you're after and let us map it out.
