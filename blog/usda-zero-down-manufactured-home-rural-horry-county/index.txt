1f:T1bdc,<p>If you&#39;ve been driving out past Conway or Loris thinking there&#39;s no way you could swing a down payment on a brand-new home, there&#39;s a program built almost exactly for where you&#39;re looking. It&#39;s called USDA Rural Development financing, and for a lot of folks buying out in the rural stretches of Horry County, it means putting nothing down. Zero. Not a smaller down payment — none at all.</p>
<p>That sounds too good to be true until you understand why it exists. The USDA wants people living and building in rural America, so it backs loans that let qualified buyers skip the down payment entirely. And much of Horry County — the parts away from the beach, out toward Conway, Loris, Longs, and the Aynor outskirts — is exactly the kind of area the program is designed for.</p>
<p>Here&#39;s how it works, and how it fits with buying a home and land together from Home Placer.</p>
<h2>What a USDA loan actually does</h2>
<p>A USDA Rural Development loan (you&#39;ll sometimes hear it called a USDA Guaranteed loan) is a mortgage backed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture. The government doesn&#39;t lend you the money directly — a regular lender does — but the USDA guarantees the loan, which makes lenders comfortable offering terms you won&#39;t find on a typical mortgage.</p>
<p>The headline feature is the one everybody cares about:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>$0 down.</strong> You can finance the full purchase price. No saving up a big down payment for years before you can buy.</li>
<li>It&#39;s built for <strong>primary residences</strong> — the home you actually live in, not a rental or vacation place.</li>
<li>It&#39;s aimed at <strong>moderate-income households</strong>, with income limits set by area and household size (more on that below).</li>
</ul>
<p>For a buyer who has steady income and decent credit but hasn&#39;t been able to stockpile a big pile of cash, USDA is often the single biggest door-opener out there. The down payment is usually the wall people hit, and this program removes it.</p>
<h2>Is your Horry County lot actually USDA-eligible?</h2>
<p>This is the part that surprises people. &quot;Rural&quot; doesn&#39;t mean you have to be in the middle of nowhere. The USDA maps eligible areas, and a lot of land around the edges of our towns qualifies even though it doesn&#39;t feel especially remote.</p>
<p>In Horry County, the outskirts of <strong>Conway, Loris, Longs, and Aynor</strong> frequently fall inside USDA-eligible zones. You can be a reasonable drive from shopping, schools, and the Grand Strand and still be on a parcel the USDA considers rural enough to back.</p>
<p>A couple of honest caveats:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Eligibility is by location, and the maps change.</strong> A specific address either qualifies or it doesn&#39;t, and the boundaries get redrawn over time. So you don&#39;t guess — you check the exact lot.</li>
<li>The areas closest to Myrtle Beach proper are less likely to qualify, while land further inland is more likely to.</li>
</ul>
<p>The good news is that this lines up neatly with where Home Placer puts homes. When we&#39;re matching you to a lot, USDA eligibility is one of the things we can look at up front, so you&#39;re not falling in love with a piece of land only to find out the financing you were counting on doesn&#39;t reach it.</p>
<h2>The income limits, in plain English</h2>
<p>USDA loans are meant for moderate-income buyers, so there&#39;s a cap on how much your household can earn and still qualify. A few things worth knowing:</p>
<ul>
<li>The limit is based on <strong>total household income</strong>, the <strong>county</strong>, and your <strong>household size</strong> — a larger household generally gets a higher allowable limit.</li>
<li>These limits are set by the USDA and updated periodically, so the right move is to have a lender run your specific numbers rather than rely on a figure you saw online.</li>
<li>Earning a little more than you expected doesn&#39;t automatically disqualify you — some deductions and adjustments factor in, which is another reason to let a lender do the math.</li>
</ul>
<p>Home Placer isn&#39;t a lender, so we won&#39;t quote you a number or pretend to. What we will do is connect you with lenders who write USDA loans every day, so you get an accurate read on where you stand instead of a guess.</p>
<h2>How USDA pairs with a Home Placer land-home package</h2>
<p>Here&#39;s where it comes together. Home Placer sells a brand-new home and the land it sits on as <strong>one package, one closing</strong>. You own the land outright — there&#39;s no lot rent, no land lease, and <strong>no HOA</strong>. We handle the permits, delivery, setup, foundation, and the utilities: well or water, septic or sewer, and power.</p>
<p>That single-package structure matters for USDA financing because the loan is on a real piece of property — a home permanently set on land you own. The homes we carry are modern, HUD-code manufactured homes from <strong>Clayton, Cavco, and Champion</strong> — drywall interiors, energy-efficient construction, and real floor plans. These aren&#39;t the &quot;trailers&quot; people picture from decades ago.</p>
<p>So the picture for an eligible buyer can look like this:</p>
<ul>
<li>A brand-new home from the low $200s, on its own lot (often around a quarter-acre, give or take).</li>
<li><strong>$0 down</strong> through USDA, if your lot and your income qualify.</li>
<li>One closing that wraps the home, the land, and the site work together.</li>
<li>A genuine warranty package behind it: a full <strong>1-year home warranty</strong>, a <strong>2-10 Home Buyers Warranty</strong> (roughly 2 years on systems like plumbing, electrical, and HVAC, and 10 years on major structural items), and a <strong>30-day walk-through</strong> after you move in.</li>
</ul>
<p>Costs vary by home, lot, and site conditions, so the only way to get real numbers is a quote on your specific build — but the financing piece is often a lot more reachable than people assume.</p>
<h2>A quick reality check before you get excited</h2>
<p>USDA is excellent, but it&#39;s not the only road, and it isn&#39;t right for everyone. If your lot doesn&#39;t fall in an eligible area, or your income is above the limit, there are still strong options — VA with $0 down for eligible veterans, FHA with as little as about 3.5% down, or conventional financing for stronger credit profiles. The smart play is to find out which doors are actually open to you before you settle on a home and a piece of land.</p>
