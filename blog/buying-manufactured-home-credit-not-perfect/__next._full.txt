1f:T1a38,<p>Maybe you&#39;ve been telling yourself for a while now that owning a home is off the table because of your credit. A late payment a few years back, some medical bills that went sideways, a number on a report that makes you wince. So you keep renting, and you keep wondering. Here&#39;s the honest truth: a credit score that isn&#39;t spotless does not automatically mean &quot;no.&quot; It just means the path looks a little different, and the right lender matters more. Plenty of people buy a brand-new home on their own land in Horry County without a flawless financial history. Let&#39;s walk through how that actually works.</p>
<h2>Your Credit Score Is One Piece, Not the Whole Picture</h2>
<p>It&#39;s easy to fixate on the three-digit number, but lenders look at a lot more than that. When they decide whether to approve a loan, they&#39;re trying to answer one basic question: can you reliably make the payments? Your score is part of that answer, but so are several other things.</p>
<p>Here&#39;s what typically goes into the decision:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Income and how steady it is.</strong> A consistent paycheck or steady self-employment history carries real weight.</li>
<li><strong>Debt-to-income ratio.</strong> This is how much of your monthly income already goes toward debts. The more breathing room you have, the better.</li>
<li><strong>Your recent payment history.</strong> Lenders care a lot about what you&#39;ve done lately. A recent stretch of on-time payments says more than something from years ago.</li>
<li><strong>Down payment and savings.</strong> Money in the bank, even a modest amount, signals stability.</li>
<li><strong>The reason behind the rough patch.</strong> A one-time hardship reads very differently than a long pattern.</li>
</ul>
<p>So if your score isn&#39;t where you&#39;d like it, take a breath. A strong, stable income and a clean recent track record can go a long way toward balancing out an imperfect past.</p>
<h2>The Loan Programs That Tend to Be More Forgiving</h2>
<p>This is where a lot of folks are pleasantly surprised. There are loan programs built specifically to help people get into homes without requiring perfect credit or a huge pile of cash. Home Placer isn&#39;t a lender, but we connect you with lenders who work with these programs every day. Here are the main ones worth knowing about.</p>
<p><strong>FHA loans.</strong> These are backed by the Federal Housing Administration and are often a good fit when your credit is still on the mend. FHA allows for a down payment as little as about 3.5% and tends to be more flexible on credit than conventional financing. For a lot of buyers, this is the doorway in.</p>
<p><strong>USDA Rural Development loans.</strong> Much of Horry County qualifies as &quot;rural&quot; for USDA purposes, especially the areas around Conway, Loris, Longs, and the outskirts of Aynor. The big draw is $0 down for eligible buyers on a primary residence. There are income limits and the property has to be in an eligible area, but if you&#39;re buying out where the land is a little more open, this one is worth a serious look.</p>
<p><strong>VA loans.</strong> If you&#39;re an eligible veteran, a VA loan offers $0 down and tends to be generous on terms. If you qualify, it&#39;s one of the strongest financing tools out there.</p>
<p><strong>Conventional loans.</strong> If your credit is stronger, conventional financing may give you more options. And if you&#39;re not there yet, it can be a goal to work toward.</p>
<p>The point is that &quot;imperfect credit&quot; covers a wide range of situations, and so do the loan programs. The right match depends on your specific picture.</p>
<h2>What You Can Do to Strengthen Your Position</h2>
<p>If you&#39;re not quite ready to apply, or you want to put yourself in the best spot before you do, there are real, practical steps that help. None of these are magic, but together they move the needle.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pull your credit reports and check them.</strong> You&#39;re entitled to free copies. Errors are more common than people think, and disputing a mistake can help.</li>
<li><strong>Pay every bill on time, every time.</strong> Recent, consistent on-time payments are one of the most powerful things you can do.</li>
<li><strong>Chip away at credit card balances.</strong> Lowering how much of your available credit you&#39;re using tends to help your score.</li>
<li><strong>Avoid opening new debt right before you apply.</strong> A new car loan or credit card can shift your ratios at the worst moment.</li>
<li><strong>Save what you can.</strong> Even a small cushion strengthens your application and gives you options.</li>
</ul>
<p>Sometimes the smartest move is simply to talk to a lender now, even if you think you&#39;re not ready. A good loan officer can look at your situation and tell you exactly what to focus on. You might be closer than you assume.</p>
<h2>Why a New Manufactured Home Fits This Conversation</h2>
<p>There&#39;s a practical reason this all comes together well with a brand-new manufactured home. Pricing starts from the low $200s, and these are genuinely well-built homes, drywall interiors, energy-efficient construction, real floor plans from builders like Clayton, Cavco, and Champion. These are not the trailers of decades past.</p>
<p>When you buy with Home Placer, you&#39;re getting the home and the land it sits on as one package, one closing. You own the land, no HOA, typically on a roughly quarter-acre lot. We handle the permits, delivery, setup, foundation, and the utilities, well or water, septic or sewer, and power. And your home comes with a full 1-year home warranty, plus a 2-10 Home Buyers Warranty (about two years of coverage on systems like plumbing, electrical, and HVAC, and ten years on major structural items), plus a 30-day walk-through after you&#39;ve settled in. A more approachable price point often means a more approachable loan, which matters a great deal when your credit is still coming together.</p>
<h2>Take the First Step Without the Pressure</h2>
<p>Nobody&#39;s credit story is the whole story, and a rough patch doesn&#39;t define what you can do next. The honest answer to &quot;can I buy a home with less-than-perfect credit?&quot; is usually &quot;let&#39;s find out together,&quot; not a flat no.</p>
