Single-Wide vs. Double-Wide: How to Choose

A plain-spoken guide to single-wide, double-wide, and multi-section manufactured homes: space, layout, cost, land needs, and financing in Horry County, SC.

If you've started shopping for a manufactured home around the Grand Strand, you've probably run into the terms "single-wide" and "double-wide" before anyone explained what they actually mean for you. They sound like jargon, and the truth is the difference is pretty simple once you cut through it. It comes down to how wide the home is, how it gets to your land, and how much room and money you're working with.

Here's the honest rundown so you can walk into the decision knowing what each one really gives you, and what it asks of your lot and your budget.

What the terms actually mean

A manufactured home is built in sections at a factory, then delivered to your land and set up on a permanent foundation. The "wide" part refers to how many sections the home comes in.

  • Single-wide — one long, narrow section. It arrives in one piece and sits as one rectangle. These are the most compact, most affordable layouts.
  • Double-wide — two sections that get joined together on-site, side by side, to make a much wider, more house-shaped footprint. This is what most people picture when they think "new manufactured home."
  • Triple-wide / multi-section — three or more sections joined together for a wide-open footprint, bigger rooms, and floor plans that rival a site-built house. Less common, but a real option if you want the space and have the land for it.

All of these are HUD-code homes, built to a federal construction standard, and the ones we carry come from Clayton, Cavco, and Champion. Modern versions have drywall, real kitchens, energy-efficient construction, and floor plans that don't feel anything like the "trailers" people remember from decades ago.

Space and layout: the real difference

This is where the choice gets practical. A single-wide is narrow, so the rooms tend to line up one after another and you walk through the home end to end. It's efficient and easy to keep up, and a well-designed single-wide can feel surprisingly open. If you want a comfortable home without a lot of extra rooms to clean and heat, it does the job.

A double-wide opens everything up. Because it's roughly twice as wide, you get:

  • Wider living areas and an open kitchen-to-living flow
  • Room for a larger primary suite, often with a walk-in closet and a bigger bath
  • Hallways and bedrooms that feel like a standard house, not a corridor

A triple-wide or multi-section takes that even further: bigger great rooms, more bedrooms, sometimes a dedicated office or split bedroom layout. If square footage and an open feel are your priority, this is the top of the range.

A simple way to think about it: single-wides are great when you want simple and budget-friendly. Double-wides are the sweet spot for buyers who want a true house feel. Multi-section is for when you want maximum room and have the land and budget to match.

What each one needs from your land

Here's the part people don't always think about up front. The wider the home, the more land you need to set it properly and meet setback rules, which are the required distances between your home and the property lines.

The good news: around here, lots are often about a quarter-acre, give or take, and that's plenty of room for a single- or double-wide in most cases. A multi-section home has a bigger footprint, so it wants a more generous lot, especially if you also want a yard, a driveway, and room for a porch or shed.

Because Home Placer sells the home and the land as one package, this is something we sort out for you. When you tell us which home you're drawn to, we make sure the lot actually fits it: setbacks, septic or sewer placement, the well or water line, power, and the driveway. You own the land outright, and there's no HOA telling you what you can and can't do with it.

Cost: where the money goes

Let's keep this honest, because exact numbers depend on the home you pick, the land, and the site work. As a rule of thumb:

  • Single-wides carry the lowest price because there's less home and a simpler set.
  • Double-wides cost more than single-wides but give you a lot more home for the money, which is why they're the most popular choice.
  • Multi-section homes sit at the top, with more material, more square footage, and a bigger set.

Our packages start from the low $200s, and that's the land plus a brand-new home, not just the box. Site costs like the foundation, utilities, and permits can vary depending on your specific lot, so the right move is to get a real quote rather than guess. We'll walk you through exactly what's included so there are no surprises.

How financing fits in

A bigger home means a bigger loan, so the single-vs-double question and your financing question are tied together. Home Placer isn't a lender — we connect you with lenders who work with these homes — but here's the lay of the land:

  • USDA Rural Development — $0 down for eligible rural areas, and much of Horry County qualifies, including the outskirts of Conway, Loris, Longs, and Aynor. Income limits apply and it has to be your primary residence.
  • VA loans — $0 down for eligible veterans.
  • FHA — as little as about 3.5% down, which keeps the upfront cost approachable.
  • Conventional — a good fit for buyers with stronger credit.

The practical takeaway: if a double-wide is the home you really want but the monthly payment stretches you, a $0-down USDA or VA loan can make it work without a big down payment. A lender can tell you what you qualify for, and we're happy to point you to one.

A quick word on peace of mind

Whichever size you choose, every new home we sell comes with a full 1-year home warranty, plus 2-10 warranty coverage that runs about 2 years on systems like plumbing, electrical, and HVAC, and 10 years on major structural items. You also get a 30-day walk-through after you move in, so anything you notice while settling in gets handled.

So which one fits you?

Start with two questions: how much room do you actually need, and what land are you working with? If you want simple and affordable, a single-wide does more than you'd expect. If you want that true open-house feel, a double-wide is the sweet spot most buyers land on. And if you want maximum space, a multi-section is there for you.
