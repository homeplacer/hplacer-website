Clayton, Cavco or Champion: Choosing Your Home Brand

An honest look at the three manufactured-home builders Home Placer carries in Horry County — Clayton, Cavco, and Champion — and how to pick the right one for you.

When folks come see us in Horry County, one of the first questions we hear is, "Which brand is best?" It's a fair question, and the honest answer is: there isn't one "best" builder. Clayton, Cavco, and Champion all build solid, well-warranted homes, and we carry all three because each one does certain things especially well. The right brand for you depends on your floor plan, your budget, and what matters most when you walk through the door.

Here's a plain-spoken rundown of what each builder brings to the table, so you can walk into our lot already knowing what to look at.

A Quick Word on What They Share

Before we split them apart, know what they have in common. Every home we place — Clayton, Cavco, or Champion — is brand-new, built to the federal HUD code, and comes with a one-year manufacturer's warranty. We pair each one with land as a single package, so there's no HOA and no hunting for a lot on your own. Homes start in the low $200s for the land-and-home package, and we work with FHA, VA, and conventional financing. So no matter which badge is on the home, you're getting new construction, a real warranty, and a clear path to financing. The differences below are about feel, layout, and finish — not about whether the home is good. They're all good.

Clayton: The Range and the Reliability

Clayton is the biggest name in the business, and that scale works in your favor. Because they build so many homes, they offer the widest spread of floor plans and price points — which is exactly why most of our buyers end up looking at a Clayton first.

The series we carry each have their own personality:

  • Horizon — a great starting point for first-time buyers who want a clean, modern home without paying for extras they don't need.
  • Giles — a step up in size and layout flexibility, popular with growing families.
  • Oxford — a little more refined in the finishes, for buyers who want the home to feel "done" the day they move in.
  • Rockwell — our roomier Clayton line, with open living areas that live large.

The thread running through all of them is consistency. Clayton's build quality is dependable lot to lot, parts and service are easy to come by, and resale tends to be straightforward because so many people know the name. If you're not sure where to start, start with Clayton — there's almost always a plan that fits.

Cavco: Style and Smart Design

Cavco is the builder we point to when someone says, "I want it to feel a little different." Cavco puts real thought into design — the way light comes into a room, how the kitchen flows into the living space, the little finish touches that make a home feel custom instead of catalog.

We carry two Cavco lines:

  • Vivid — bright, open, and modern, with layouts built around how people actually live today. Good kitchen footprints, comfortable great rooms.
  • Atmos — a bit more elevated in style, for buyers who care about the look and feel of the finishes and want some personality in the design.

If you and your family tend to gather in the kitchen, host on weekends, or just want a home that feels fresh and current the moment you step inside, Cavco is worth a hard look. You're paying for thoughtful design, and with Cavco you can feel it.

Champion: Built Tough, Built Tall

Champion is our pick for buyers who lead with two questions: "How well is it built?" and "How does it feel inside?" Champion answers both.

  • Iron Clad — the name says it. This line leans into durability and a sturdy, well-put-together feel. It's a great match for buyers who want a home that feels rock-solid underfoot and plan to stay put for the long haul.
  • Dutch Elite — this is the showstopper for a lot of our visitors, mostly because of the nine-foot ceilings. Most homes in this class run eight-foot ceilings, and that extra foot completely changes how a room feels. Spaces feel bigger, brighter, and more open without your having to buy more square footage. Walk into a Dutch Elite and you notice it immediately.

If a home that feels open and substantial matters to you, put Champion on your list — especially the Dutch Elite if those ceilings catch your eye.

So How Do You Actually Choose?

Here's the way we'd coach a friend through it. Don't start with the brand — start with you.

  • On a tight budget or buying your first home? Begin with Clayton's Horizon. It gives you new construction and a warranty without paying for extras you may not use.
  • Want the home to feel designed and modern? Walk a Cavco Vivid or Atmos. Pay attention to the kitchen and the natural light.
  • Want it to feel big and solid? Stand inside a Champion Dutch Elite and look up. Those nine-foot ceilings sell themselves.
  • Not sure? That's the most common answer, and it's fine. Come see two or three in person. Ten minutes of walking through homes tells you more than ten hours of reading.

One thing we'll never do is push you toward a brand because it's easy for us. Our job is to match you to the home that fits your family and your budget — and since we carry all three, we have no reason to steer you. Whichever badge ends up on your door, it comes with land, a warranty, and a price we'll quote you straight.

Come Walk a Few

Floor plans and photos only go so far. The real difference between these three brands is something you feel when you stand inside them — the ceiling height, the kitchen flow, the way the light lands. We'd love to show you in person, talk through your budget, and find your exact number (we don't do guesswork on price).
