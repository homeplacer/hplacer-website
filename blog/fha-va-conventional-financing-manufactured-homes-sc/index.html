FHA vs VA vs Conventional: Financing a Manufactured Home in SC

FHA, VA, and conventional loans for a manufactured home on land in Horry County, SC — down payments, who each fits, and how Home Placer connects you to lenders.

One of the first questions we hear from folks here in Horry County is some version of: "Can I actually get a real mortgage on a new manufactured home?" The short answer is yes. When you buy a brand-new Clayton, Cavco, or Champion home placed on land — titled as real property, the way we do it — you have the same main financing roads open to you as someone buying a stick-built house: FHA, VA, and conventional. The trick is knowing which road fits your situation.

We're not a lender, and we won't pretend to be. What we do is help you understand your options in plain English, then connect you with lenders who finance manufactured-home-on-land packages every day. Below is the honest rundown we'd give a neighbor.

Why "home on land" changes everything

Before we compare loans, here's the piece that matters most. A manufactured home parked in a rental community on a leased lot is usually financed with a chattel (personal property) loan — shorter terms, higher rates. That is not what Home Placer sells.

We pair a new home with the land as one package, and the home is permanently set and titled as real property. That distinction is what unlocks FHA, VA, and conventional mortgages with the longer terms and friendlier rates you'd want. So as you read on, know that everything here assumes the land-and-home setup we specialize in across Conway, Loris, Longs, Aynor, and the wider Grand Strand.

FHA loans: the first-timer's friend

FHA loans are backed by the Federal Housing Administration, and they're often the easiest door to walk through — especially for first-time and budget-conscious buyers.

  • Down payment: as low as 3.5% with a qualifying credit score.
  • Credit: more forgiving than conventional. Lower scores can still work, sometimes with a slightly larger down payment.
  • Best for: first-time buyers, anyone who's rebuilding credit, or folks who simply don't have a big pile of cash saved.

The trade-off is mortgage insurance. FHA loans carry an upfront and an ongoing mortgage insurance premium, which adds to your monthly payment and typically stays for the life of the loan. For a lot of our buyers, that's a fair price to pay for getting into a brand-new home now instead of renting for three more years. FHA also has specific requirements for how the home is installed and foundationed — the good news is that the way Home Placer sets homes is built to meet them.

VA loans: earned, and hard to beat

If you've served, this is the benefit you earned, and it's genuinely one of the best deals in housing. We've got a lot of military and veteran families along the Grand Strand, and we love helping them use this.

  • Down payment: $0 down for eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and many surviving spouses.
  • Mortgage insurance: none. That's a real monthly savings versus FHA.
  • Best for: anyone with VA eligibility — full stop. If you qualify, this almost always beats the alternatives.

There is a one-time VA funding fee (some disabled veterans are exempt), and the home and land have to meet VA's standards. VA financing of a manufactured home is absolutely doable when it's set as real property on its own land, which is exactly our model. If you've got a Certificate of Eligibility or think you might qualify, tell us early so we point you to a VA-savvy lender.

Conventional loans: when your file is strong

Conventional loans aren't government-backed; they follow Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac guidelines. They reward buyers with solid credit and a bit more to put down.

  • Down payment: commonly 5% and up; programs exist as low as 3% for some buyers.
  • Mortgage insurance: required if you put down less than 20%, but it can drop off once you've built enough equity — unlike FHA's. That long-term savings is the big draw.
  • Best for: buyers with good-to-strong credit who want to ditch mortgage insurance down the road, or who are putting more money down.

Fannie Mae's MH Advantage and Freddie Mac's CHOICEHome programs specifically support qualifying manufactured homes with terms that look a lot like a regular mortgage. Not every home or buyer fits, but when it works, conventional can be the lowest long-run cost of the three.

A quick side-by-side

  • Lowest down payment: VA (often $0), then FHA (3.5%), then conventional (3–5%).
  • Easiest credit: FHA, then VA, then conventional.
  • No mortgage insurance: VA wins; conventional can shed it later; FHA generally keeps it.
  • Who it fits: VA for those who served; FHA for first-timers and thinner credit; conventional for stronger files chasing long-term savings.

There's no universal "best." The right loan depends on your credit, your cash, your eligibility, and your goals. A good lender runs the numbers three ways and shows you the real monthly figure.
