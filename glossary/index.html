Manufactured home glossary

Buying a home comes with a lot of jargon. Here are the terms that actually matter, explained simply.
Manufactured home
A home built entirely in a factory to the federal HUD code, then transported to and installed on a site. Today's manufactured homes are energy-efficient and built to strict national standards.
HUD code
The federal construction and safety standard (administered by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development) that all manufactured homes must meet. It governs design, strength, energy efficiency, and fire safety.
Modular home
A factory-built home constructed to the same state/local building codes as a site-built house, then assembled on a permanent foundation. Different code path than a manufactured home.
Single-wide
A manufactured home built and transported as one section. Efficient and affordable, typically 14–18 feet wide.
Double-wide
A manufactured home built in two sections and joined on site, giving a wider, more open floor plan — commonly 24–32 feet wide.
Land-home package
Buying a new home and the land it sits on together as a single purchase, with one price and one closing. Home Placer's core offering.
Chattel loan
A personal-property loan used for a home without the land (such as one in a rented community). Shorter terms and higher rates than a real-property mortgage — not what you use for a land-home package.
Real property
A home permanently affixed to land you own, financed and taxed like a traditional house. Land-home packages are titled as real property.
FHA loan
A government-backed mortgage with flexible credit guidelines and low down payments (often 3.5%), popular with first-time and budget-minded buyers.
VA loan
A mortgage backed by the Department of Veterans Affairs for eligible service members and veterans, often with no down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance.
Foundation / pad
The prepared base the home is set on. Manufactured homes on land are set on a permanent foundation, anchored with tie-downs.
Tie-downs
Anchoring straps and hardware that secure a manufactured home to its foundation against wind — important in the coastal Carolinas.
Skirting
The material enclosing the space between the bottom of the home and the ground, improving appearance, energy efficiency, and protection from the elements.
Set / setup
The process of placing the home on its foundation, joining sections (for multi-section homes), leveling, anchoring, and connecting it to utilities.
Perc test
A soil test that determines whether a lot can support a septic system, and what kind — a key step when placing a home on rural land.
Decor package
A coordinated set of interior finishes (cabinets, flooring, colors) offered by the manufacturer to personalize a floor plan.
